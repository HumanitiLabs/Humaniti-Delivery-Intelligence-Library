The 7 Constraints

Constraint 01 — Reactive Resource Allocation
Assessment Pillar: Delivery Capacity  |  HUMANITI Concept: Resource Friction™
What It Is
Resource decisions are made in response to crises, not in anticipation of them. By the time a staffing gap is identified, the project is already delayed, the delivery team is already overloaded, and the solution — whatever it is — will cost more than it should have. This is not a hiring problem. It is a planning infrastructure problem.
Symptoms
You are experiencing this constraint if:

You discover resourcing gaps when projects are already delayed, not before they begin.
Staffing conversations are triggered by client complaints or missed milestones, not by pipeline signals.
The same two or three people are always pulled from other projects to rescue the one that's at risk.
Your delivery team has no structured model for what capacity looks like in 60 or 90 days.
Resource allocation decisions happen in meetings, not through a planning system.

Business Impact
Impact AreaWhat It CostsDelivery Capacity15–25% of billable capacity consumed by reactive reshuffling, emergency ramp-up, and delayed project starts.MarginLast-minute staffing carries a premium cost. Delayed starts extend project timelines and erode fixed-price margins.Leadership TimeSenior leaders pulled into resource firefighting weekly. Strategic delivery decisions crowd out project-level problem-solving.ScalabilityThe firm cannot absorb new project volume because available capacity is never accurately known.

Firms scoring below 12 on Pillar 1 (Delivery Capacity) — specifically on Q3 (Resource allocation is proactive rather than reactive) — report capacity surprises as a near-constant operational reality. The assessment identifies this as Resource Friction™: the systemic cost of demand-unaware resourcing.


Constraint 02 — Delivery Visibility Collapse
Assessment Pillar: Delivery Visibility  |  HUMANITI Concept: Delivery Visibility™
What It Is
Leadership has no consistent, real-time view across the delivery portfolio. Project health is known through escalation calls, informal check-ins, and subjective PM narrative — all of which are lagging signals. By the time a risk reaches leadership awareness, it has typically been active for weeks. What leadership sees is not delivery reality. It is a filtered, delayed, relationship-mediated version of it.
Symptoms
You are experiencing this constraint if:

You find out projects are in trouble from client calls, not from internal reporting.
Weekly status meetings are dominated by narrative updates, not objective health indicators.
Leadership has a general sense of "how things are going" but no structured portfolio view.
Escalations consistently arrive as surprises, not as anticipated escalation events.
Different projects use different status formats, making cross-portfolio comparison impossible.

Business Impact

Governance interventions applied too late to prevent damage.Client RelationshipsFirst visibility of project problems is often a client complaint — the worst possible moment to learn.ScalabilityLeadership cannot manage a growing portfolio without a visibility infrastructure. Span of control collapses above a threshold.
📊 Assessment Signal

Firms scoring below 12 on Pillar 2 (Delivery Visibility) report that leadership's first signal of a troubled project is almost universally a client complaint, not an internal flag. The assessment identifies Delivery Visibility Collapse as the most common single-pillar driver of escalation volume across the ICP.


Constraint 03 — Escalation Overload
Assessment Pillar: Delivery Governance  |  HUMANITI Concept: Escalation Velocity™
What It Is
Senior leaders are the default escalation point for delivery issues that should be resolved at the project or practice level. Escalation paths may exist on paper — but in practice, delivery teams bypass structured governance and take issues directly to the top. Leadership becomes the delivery infrastructure. Every unresolved issue at the delivery layer becomes a leadership problem.
Symptoms
You are experiencing this constraint if:

Senior leaders spend more time resolving delivery issues than they do on strategy or business development.
Escalation paths are documented but rarely used — delivery teams default to calling the most senior person they know.
The same categories of issue — scope disputes, client relationship tension, resource conflicts — escalate repeatedly with no structural resolution.
Leadership meetings are dominated by reactive problem-solving rather than forward-looking governance.
No defined threshold exists for what should and should not be escalated to senior leadership.

Business Impact
Impact AreaWhat It CostsLeadership TimeSenior leaders consuming 30–45% of available time on reactive escalation management that should be handled below them.Delivery ThroughputDecision latency increases as issues queue for leadership attention. Projects stall waiting for escalation resolution.Governance IntegrityEscalation overload signals broken governance, not high delivery complexity. The structure itself is the constraint.CultureDelivery teams learn that escalation is the fastest resolution path — reinforcing the behavior that creates the overload.
📊 Assessment Signal

Firms scoring below 12 on Pillar 3 (Delivery Governance) — specifically on Q2 and Q3 — consistently report escalation volume as their primary leadership time drain. The assessment quantifies this as Escalation Velocity™: the rate at which unresolved issues accelerate upward through the organization.


Constraint 04 — Scope Creep Without Margin Recovery
Assessment Pillar: Margin Protection  |  HUMANITI Concept: Margin Leakage™
What It Is
Scope changes are accommodated during delivery without formal change control, margin impact analysis, or client repricing. Delivery teams absorb expanded scope as a relationship investment — because enforcing change control feels uncomfortable, and because no governance structure exists to back them up when they try. The firm delivers more. The client pays for the original scope. The margin disappears quietly.
Symptoms
You are experiencing this constraint if:

Projects close with delivery effort significantly higher than originally estimated, but billing unchanged.
Change requests are managed through email exchanges and verbal agreements, not formal change orders.
PMs feel unable to enforce scope boundaries without risking the client relationship.
Post-project profitability is consistently below contract-stage projections, with no clear explanation.
Leadership is aware that scope creep is a problem but has no system to quantify or govern it.

Business Impact
Impact AreaWhat It CostsMargin20–35% of actual delivery scope is unpriced expansion. Projects that appear successful are often margin-negative.CapacityUncontrolled scope expansion consumes delivery capacity that was allocated to other projects.Pricing IntegrityChronic scope absorption trains clients to expect unlimited delivery within the original price.Leadership ClarityNo accurate picture of project profitability during delivery. Margin issues are discovered at close, not corrected mid-project.
📊 Assessment Signal

Firms scoring below 12 on Pillar 4 (Margin Protection) — specifically on Q2 (Scope changes are controlled) and Q1 (Project profitability is actively monitored) — frequently discover on project closeout that 20–35% of actual scope was uncontrolled expansion that was never billed. The assessment identifies this as Margin Leakage™.


Constraint 05 — Key-Person Dependency
Assessment Pillar: Resource Intelligence  |  HUMANITI Concept: Delivery Debt™
What It Is
Delivery capability is concentrated in 2–4 individuals. Critical knowledge — client context, architectural decisions, escalation relationships, delivery methodology — lives in people's heads and is not documented, not transferred, and not replaceable on short notice. The firm's delivery capacity is as resilient as its most critical individual's availability. Which means it is not resilient at all.
Symptoms
You are experiencing this constraint if:

When a key consultant is unavailable, specific projects stall with no clear internal replacement.
The firm cannot take on new projects without involving the same senior individuals every time.
Clients request specific individuals by name and express concern when those individuals are unavailable.
Knowledge transfer between senior and junior delivery staff is informal and inconsistent.
Attrition of any one of 2–3 individuals would create a delivery crisis with no structural contingency.

Business Impact
Impact AreaWhat It CostsDelivery ResilienceSingle-point-of-failure risk across the portfolio. One resignation event can trigger simultaneous project disruption.ScalabilityThe firm cannot scale delivery without cloning its key individuals. Hiring does not translate into delivery capacity.Retention RiskKey individuals know they are irreplaceable — which increases leverage and attrition vulnerability simultaneously.MarginSenior consultant time consumed on delivery execution that should be handled at a more leveraged level.
📊 Assessment Signal

Firms scoring below 12 on Pillar 5 (Resource Intelligence) — specifically on Q4 (Key-person dependency is low) — cannot name a structured process for any of their three most critical delivery decisions. The answer is always a person's name. The assessment identifies this as Delivery Debt™: the accumulation of operational risk deferred through individual heroics.


Constraint 06 — Project Manager Overload
Assessment Pillar: Delivery Capacity  |  HUMANITI Concept: Delivery Debt™
What It Is
Project managers are carrying more concurrent projects than governance structures or quality standards can support. They compensate through longer hours, informal coordination, and personal relationship management — masking a structural capacity problem until delivery quality deteriorates. The firm mistakes heroic effort for organizational capability. It is not. It is a quality ceiling that the firm will eventually hit.
Symptoms
You are experiencing this constraint if:

PMs are managing 5 or more concurrent projects with no documented prioritization model.
PM availability is the primary constraint on new project intake, not consultant availability.
PM performance varies significantly across the portfolio with no standardized quality floor.
Senior PMs are performing tasks that should be handled by junior team members due to capacity compression.
PM attrition is elevated, and exit interviews cite workload as the primary driver.

Business Impact
Impact AreaWhat It CostsDelivery QualityQuality degrades proportionally to PM overload — silently, until a client relationship breaks or a project fails publicly.PM AttritionOverloaded PMs are your highest flight-risk employees. Replacement cost and knowledge loss are both severe.Client SatisfactionClients experience PM overload as reduced responsiveness, missed communications, and declining project leadership quality.ScalabilityThe PM layer becomes the delivery bottleneck. The firm cannot grow without either overloading existing PMs or hiring at cost.
📊 Assessment Signal

Firms scoring below 12 on Pillar 1 Q4 (Project managers are not overloaded) have PMs managing 4–7 concurrent projects with no documented prioritization model. The assessment identifies PM overload as a secondary form of Delivery Debt™ — structural capacity deferred through individual effort.


Constraint 07 — Demand Forecasting Absence
Assessment Pillar: Resource Intelligence  |  HUMANITI Concept: Resource Friction™
What It Is
The firm cannot predict its resourcing requirements beyond confirmed, signed contracts. Pipeline signals do not translate into resourcing decisions until contracts are executed — at which point the lead time to deploy the right resource is already a problem. Sales and delivery operate as sequential functions, not as an integrated planning system. Every new project starts the resourcing clock from zero.
Symptoms
You are experiencing this constraint if:

No structured model exists for converting pipeline stage and probability into projected resource demand.
Hiring decisions are made reactively — after contracts are signed and delivery pressure is already building.
Leadership cannot answer the question: "What does our delivery capacity look like in 90 days?"
New projects consistently launch under-resourced, requiring emergency allocation from existing projects.
Sales and delivery leadership do not have a joint capacity planning rhythm.

Business Impact
Impact AreaWhat It CostsDelivery Start QualityProjects launch under-resourced. Time to productive delivery is 3–6 weeks longer than necessary.MarginEmergency hiring and last-minute staffing carry cost premiums that directly erode project margin.Sales CredibilityDelivery cannot commit to start dates with confidence. Sales closes deals on timelines delivery cannot honor.ScalabilityGrowth requires predictable resourcing. Without demand forecasting, growth creates compounding delivery risk.
📊 Assessment Signal

Firms scoring below 12 on Pillar 5 Q3 (Demand forecasting exists) cannot answer: "What does your delivery capacity look like in 90 days?" The answer is always speculative, never structured. The assessment identifies this as Resource Friction™: the operational cost of demand-unaware resource planning.
